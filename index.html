X_eTaL on GPUs

X_eTaL states a computation as whole-array operations, which already expose their parallelism. This repository runs the same typed X_eTaL programs on GPUs through OpenCL C 1.2, on old and new cards alike (a Kepler Quadro and Maxwell and Pascal Teslas that the newest CUDA toolkits retire, Turing to Blackwell, and Apple silicon), and proves every answer against the X_eTaL evaluator. The goal is the concept, not speed.

What runs today

X_eTaL source, lowered. Every example program is lowered from its own .xtl by this repository's translator, which takes the program's Core (libraries included) from X_eTaL's own crates and builds the program the device runs; the checks prove each one gives the evaluator's output, exactly on the interpreter and within f32's tolerance on the GPU. The hand-written twins that stood in until 2026-10-13 were the translator's oracle and are gone. X_eTaL-ML's own programs run the same way, unchanged: its benchmarks, the world model, the digit reader, the denoiser and the tiny GPT, each lowered from its source (docs/ml-results.md); what the device cannot take, the evaluator computes. A second backend of the same IR, Mojo (components/mojo), runs the elementwise and reduction demos on the M1 Max through Metal since 2026-10-16; on the RTX 5060 Ti eleven demos now pass, including the tiny network, top-k, axis folds and matrix multiplication (2026-10-23). The lowering belongs to X_eTaL in the end (asks G1 and G2).

The macro, expanded

The framework: a macro hands a function's text to a runner. In accelerate.xtl each accelerated function is one definition, written as any X_eTaL function; below each, what xetal expand makes of it (generated here from the pinned xetal-x, the expansion the runner sees). Read the expansion from the right, as X_eTaL evaluates: the text goes to the runner with the calling file and the arguments' kinds, the arguments are bound, the program runs on the device, the result is read back; gp:a_fter returns its left after its right. The runner lowers the text for the arguments' shapes when they are bound and keeps the session. The macro's page.

Measured

One X_eTaL program, offload.xtl, on two GPUs so far: an Apple M1 Max (OpenCL 1.2, this Mac) and an RTX 5060 Ti (16 GB, an Arch Linux machine). Each GPU is compared with the evaluator on its own host, not with the other card. The GPU side includes the bridge, which carries arrays as text.

WorkEvaluator on the MacApple M1 MaxEvaluator on the Arch hostRTX 5060 Ti
512 by 512 product14545 ms462 ms (31 times faster)24994 ms979 ms (25 times faster)
a million items, elementwise, summed188 ms2447 ms (13 times slower)254 ms1440 ms (5.7 times slower)

Products gain even through the bridge; elementwise work does not. Why, and which macros and extensions are justified: the analysis.
